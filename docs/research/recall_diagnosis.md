# Why legitimate speech is missed, and what was changed (ADR-0022 follow-up)

_Generated by `tools/bench/recall_report.py` from `reports/ai/recall-*.json`; do not edit by hand. **PRELIMINARY: synthetic.** `tests/action_bench_v1` is written by one author family and labelled by construction; the safety panel and the regression sentences are builder-written; none of it is independent evidence, and speech-based scoring stays OFF._

## What was asked, how the evidence was kept honest

* Every legitimate row (status actual, categories paraphrase and asr_error: 140 per split) is traced through the stages of the path in production order (transcript, context, candidate generation, critical-mass cap, per-act threshold, critical-content cue, second reader, assertion layer, status and context rules, and finally the ENGINE's evidence store, run by the CLI command `evidence-check`, not by a Python copy of its rules). `tools/bench/recall_diagnosis.py`.
* The causes and the fixes were chosen on the **dev** split and on the safety panel. The candidate set and the selection rule were frozen in `tools/bench/recall_frozen.json` before the held-out splits (`test`, `test2`) were scored; they were then scored once per candidate (`recall-eval-<split>.json`, locked). The causes on test and test2 below were traced after the freeze, to confirm the dev picture, not to choose anything.
* **What was seen:** the whole safety panel (including challenge v6) was used when the cap value was chosen, so none of it is unseen; the AI-judged v2 counts were printed with the dev run, so v2 test is corroboration, not a held-out claim. The only unseen data are the action_bench test and test2 splits. After the freeze, assertion-layer holes were found by hand-written counterexamples and closed; the shipped configuration was re-scored on test and test2 as a labelled second look (`recall-confirm-<split>.json`) and accepted exactly the same sentences as the frozen run.

## 2. A downstream rejection the benchmark did not show

`content/assessment/mapping.json` carried one engine threshold, 0.8 for every act, marked "the guide's example, replaced by the calibration export when one exists". The classifier's own calibrated thresholds range from 0.15 to 0.75 and no export was ever made, so the engine's evidence store refused evidence the gate had credited. Earlier benchmark pages counted what the service OFFERED; these numbers count what the ENGINE ACCEPTS:

| Split | Offered by the service (baseline) | Accepted by the engine | Refused (LOW_CONFIDENCE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| dev | 83 | 82 | 1 |
| test | 66 | 63 | 3 |
| test2 | 69 | 67 | 2 |

Fix (`tools/content/build_scoring.py`): the engine's per-act thresholds are the head's calibrated thresholds floored to three decimals, so the engine never refuses what the gate credited at its own threshold and never accepts below it. Isolated effect: dev 82 to 83, test 63 to 66, test2 67 to 69. It matters more once the cap is relaxed: with the cap at 0.05 and the placeholder kept, the engine would refuse 8 (dev), 7 (test) and 6 (test2) correct offers.

The margin: the nearest of those has critical mass 0.0757, 1.51 times the new cap. **Rule used (frozen):** Two separate changes. (1) The engine's per-act evidence thresholds are the classifier head's calibrated thresholds floored to three decimals (mapping.json said the 0.8 default was a placeholder to be replaced by this export); adopted because it removes a refusal of evidence the gate had credited and cannot admit anything the gate refused. (2) The head's critical-mass cap moves from 0.03 to the LARGEST value of the grid {0.03, 0.04, 0.05} at which (a) no sentence of the safety panel is credited or accepted (tools/bench/recall_safety_panel.py: 1,694 sentences of evasive critical content and non-performed speech in the most permissive context) and (b) the nearest sentence the cap alone blocks (critical mass 0.0757) is at least 1.5 times the cap. 0.05 is the largest value that satisfies both (0.0757 / 0.05 = 1.51).

**Caveat.** The head's own tuning table recorded one misroute at 0.05 on the AI-judged v2 dev split when it was built; on the saved head it re-measures as 0 of 105 (revision block in `content/assessment/act_head_hybrid.json`). The panel is synthetic and builder-written; a margin of 1.5 times is not a guarantee. Rollback is one value (`tools/bench/recall_frozen.json`).

## 5. Causes examined and deliberately NOT changed

* **Regular-expression cue false positives (dev: 4 rows, e.g. "I would like a chest x ray requested", "I would like the senior doctor to see him").** The cue `i would like (the|a|someone)` is broad, but these sentences are genuinely ambiguous with an SBAR recommendation ("I would like the anaesthetist to review before theatre", an approved positive example of a critical act). Narrowing a critical-content floor on two synthetic sentences would be tuning to the benchmark; left as a conservative rejection.
* **ASR-style fused words ("onthe", "theporter", "feelanxious").** The benchmark's seeded damage drops spaces; real recognisers (Whisper) substitute words, they rarely fuse them. A word-segmentation repair would exploit that artifact; not done.
* **Context-constrained re-ranking (promote the best ELIGIBLE act when the head's top act is not eligible).** Examined on dev: 2 missed rows have a top act that is not eligible in the row's state, and 0 of them would be reachable at the shipped cap, so the change would recover nothing on dev while adding a way for a critical sentence to be turned into an ordinary act. Not done.
* **Per-act or ratio-based caps, and the second head's critical mass.** The TF-IDF head's critical mass does not separate the blocked legitimate rows from the unsafe ones (the ranges overlap completely above the nearest unsafe sentence); a per-act cap would be fitted to a handful of unsafe sentences. Not done.
* **Per-act thresholds (BELOW_THRESHOLD, e.g. REPORT_FAULT 0.747).** Calibrated for credited precision 0.8 on a different dev set (ADR-0016); not touched.
* **Another model or technique.** None was run: no diagnosed failure needs one that a targeted experiment could change. The remaining category (a legitimate sentence whose critical mass equals that of unsafe speech) needs a better critical-content signal; that is a question for the independent set (EX-01).

## 6. Holes in the assertion layer found by counterexamples, and closed

For 14 acts, hand-written legitimate paraphrases were paired with a denial, a plan, a hypothetical, a report, another speaker and a broken-off sentence (84 counterexamples). The layer before the fixes (`2f9aa0a`) caught 64 of them; the classifier refused some of the rest by luck and the critical-mass cap hid others. Found:

* **Other speakers.** Four patterns each carried its own short list of roles, so "The technician will report the monitor fault" and "The radiographer will explain the scan to you" were credited. One shared list of ward, theatre, laboratory and imaging roles now serves all of them.
* **Plans.** "I'll order the ECG after the handover", "I'll call the senior in a few minutes", "I'll explain the operation once you have signed" were credited: the delay markers were a handful of words. They are widened to a class (after the/this/that, in a few minutes, tomorrow, once I/you have, and similar). "I will tell you when it is in" and "I will call you when the porter arrives" are read-backs and keep their credit.
* **Conditionals.** "If the suction were broken I would report it", "If you asked how long it takes, I would say about an hour", "If I asked you to say it back, could you?" were credited. A sentence that opens with if and states what the speaker would do, or asks what the listener would, is now hypothetical. "What would you like me to explain?" and "If you have any questions, I will answer them" keep their credit.
* **Denials with "say".** "I didn't say you looked worried" was not a denial (only "haven't" and similar were).

## 8. What this does and does not show

* **Recall genuinely improved on the held-out synthetic splits without a measured safety cost:** accepted right sentences 63 to 76 of 140 (test) and 67 to 79 of 140 (test2), credited precision 76/76 and 79/79, no critical, non-assertive or out-of-window evidence accepted, no panel sentence accepted. Part of the gain is the engine threshold (a downstream rejection), part the cap.
* **It is not independent evidence.** The sentences are builder-written; the safety margin of the cap is 1.51 times against 9 sentences; the panel was used to choose the value. The independent human-labelled set (`docs/testing/independent_labelling_protocol.md`, EX-01) must be scored with this exact configuration before any claim: it is the only data that can say whether 0.05 is safe on real trainee speech and whether the 32 to 46 percent of legitimate sentences still missed are recoverable.
* `speech_interpretation` stays OFF and SKIPPED: its suites read the independent result file, which does not exist. Nothing here enables live scoring. No device check was run (Sannan's DC-08 and DC-09 are NOT RUN).
