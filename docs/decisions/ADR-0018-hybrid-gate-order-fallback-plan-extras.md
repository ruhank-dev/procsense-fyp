# ADR-0018 — Hybrid credit head, two-stage order fallback, plan extras and trap writer (fourth session)

> **Superseded as architecture by [ADR-0025](ADR-0025-gemma-first-speech-evaluation.md) (21 Oct 2026).** The speech part: the classifier, gate, cap, cue, assertion and interpretation machinery of the speech part was retired and removed; the record and its measurements are kept as history. The spoken-order fallback and plan extras parts of this record are unaffected.

**Date:** 18 October 2026 (fourth session) · **Status:** Accepted for the engineering; every feature stays OFF (no acceptance met) · **Related:** ADR-0005, ADR-0016, ADR-0017, DT-224

## Context
The third-session credit gate was safe on its development sets but credited only about a third of the ordinary sentences. The spoken-order model fallback read back the right item for 70.5 percent of a development set against a 90 to 95 percent target. Four composer capabilities (A4.c2, A5.c2, A7.c2, A8.c2) had no engine hook.

## Decisions
1. **Classifier.** The first reader of the credit gate is a hybrid head (sentence embedding and TF-IDF features in one regression, `server/classify/hybrid.py`); the TF-IDF head stays the second reader and every guard is unchanged. Legitimate credit rose 33.1 to 39.6 percent (v2 test) and 33.8 to 38.2 percent (v3) without critical false credit on any development set. The frozen set v5 (scored once) still showed 1 of 48 critical sentences and 1 of 12 non-assertive sentences credited (the third-session gate also credited 1 of 48), so the target of zero is NOT met on fresh data; the two sentences were fixed afterwards and added to the regression fixture, which makes v5 a development set. The classifier stays OFF; a new frozen set (v6) and the independent set (EX-01) are the way forward. A guard in `safety_gate.py` now refuses to write results when the embedder dropped rows (a saturated Ollama gave invalid numbers once).
2. **Spoken orders.** *Correction (19 October 2026):* the fourth-session text said the fallback asked the kind first and gave each item a note built from the content. That was NOT what ran: the committed `map()` made one call, with the prompt `orders.map@2` (which describes a name and a note) and only the generic kind words as notes, a confidence floor of 0.9, and never called the kind stage or the content notes it contained. The figures reported then (development set 70.5 to 83.6 percent; frozen second set 76.4 percent correct, 18.2 percent wrong, 1 of 10 non-orders offered, about 5 s per call) are real measurements of that code and stand as such. They do not measure the design that text described.
   *Fifth session.* The design was then built as described and extended: kind stage; notes made from the content (tests: kind and analytes; medicines: kind and effects; places: capabilities); a deterministic re-ranking of the items of the chosen kind by the rare words they share with the sentence; the choice is accepted only if the re-ranker's first item agrees or a second call with the items in reverse order picks the same item; disagreement becomes a question between the two items (nothing is read back as an order); the stated confidence is not used for the decision. Synonym table: two entries pointed at ids that do not exist (`coag`, `glucose`) and are fixed, about 45 entries added (a test checks that every synonym names something that exists). Grammar: 'take her along to the CT scanner' and 'send him down to the CT department' are transport, not a scan order.
   *Measured, fifth session.* Development set (its failures were analysed, so it proves nothing about generalisation): 59.0 percent correct, 4.9 percent wrong, 36 percent sent to the menu, 4 questions between two items (all containing the right one). **Frozen third set (66 rows written before the re-ranker and the agreement rule existed, scored once, same author family): 58.9 percent correct, 10.7 percent wrong, 3 questions (2 with the right item), 1 of 10 non-orders offered, nothing released without a yes, about 4.5 s per call (p95 8 s).** The agreement rule trades recall for fewer wrong read-backs; it does not reach the target (90 to 95 percent correct, at most 5 percent wrong, 3 s). `order_fallback` stays OFF. The model cannot be made reliable enough at these near neighbours with a 4B model and no further data; what would move it is in P3-02.
3. **Plan extras and trap writer.** Events, what-if branches (A8) and interactions (A4) are chosen by the model only among candidates made by code (eligible events; knowledge-base-linked problem pairs), validated by `plan_extras.validate`, carried in `provenance.plan`, and read by the engine through `SessionOptions.PlannedEvents` (practice mode only; replay-safe). The trap writer rewords only wrong options, keeps ids, flags and consequences, reverts any wording that lowers trap fairness and records `trap_wording`. Measured with the real 4B on the library (reports/ai/plan-extras-eval.json, trap-writer-eval.json): no ineligible event and no refused proposal on 53 cases; interactions on 24 of 53 cases, which equals the number of cases that have a knowledge-base pair at all (the model chose every candidate, so it adds nothing over the code), against the plan's 80 percent; 24 of 98 wrong options reworded, none lowered trap fairness. Relevance and plausibility are human ratings (EX-03) and are not measured. The gates `plan_extras` and `trap_writer` are OFF.
4. **KB extraction** moved to the 4B (ADR-0017).

## Consequences
Nothing is switched on by this change. The engine gains one optional header field. Claims stay labelled synthetic or proxy.

## Revisit if
A frozen v6 set shows zero false credit; an independent set exists; a larger shortlist model or a fine-tuned mapper reaches the order target on a fresh frozen set; raters judge the extras or the reworded traps better than the baseline.

## Addendum, 19 October 2026 (fifth session)
* **Classifier.** The reasons for refused credit on 1,029 ordinary development sentences were counted (`tools/bench/false_reject_analysis.py`): the critical-mass guard removes about a third of them (333), a wrong act is named for 91, denials and hypotheticals for 58, a threshold for 42; the second reader only 17. A head cannot recover what a guard removes on purpose, so the legitimate recall stayed where it was (37 to 38 percent on the v2 and v3 splits, 70 percent on the fresh v6). The heads were rebuilt with the v4 critical rows as hard negatives; the cap of the hybrid head is the largest of a wider grid with no held-out critical false credit (0.03). **Frozen challenge set v6 (130 rows, written before any change, scored once, synthetic, same author family): 0 of 48 evasive critical sentences credited, 0 of 12 denials/hypotheticals/reports credited, 49 of 70 ordinary credited at 98 percent precision.** The shipped head of the session-3 start credited 3 of 48 on the same set. This meets the synthetic zero-false-credit target for the first time on a fresh set; it is not independent evidence (the interval of 0 in 48 reaches 7.4 percent; no person labelled a row), so the classifier stays OFF until EX-01. v6 is now a development set for any further change; the next frozen set is v7. `safety_gate.py` now refuses to write a run in which any variant scored a different number of rows than the set holds.
* **Spoken orders.** See the correction in decision 2: the fifth-session mapper (kind stage, content notes, re-ranking, agreement of two readings, a question when they disagree) scored 58.9 percent correct and 10.7 percent wrong on a frozen third set (66 rows). It stays OFF.
* **9B.** Retired, ADR-0019.
