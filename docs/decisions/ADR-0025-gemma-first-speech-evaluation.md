# ADR-0025 — Gemma is the semantic evaluator of trainee speech; the simulator's state and evidence are authoritative; a deterministic engine turns both into the score

**Date:** 21 October 2026 · **Status:** **ACCEPTED and APPROVED on 22 Oct 2026** (final acceptance below; owner decision of 21 Oct 2026: "ASR → Gemma semantic evaluation → authoritative simulator state/evidence → deterministic score calculation → VR feedback" is the target architecture; step p1-ai-language-s22). **Supersedes, as architecture,** ADR-0012, ADR-0013, ADR-0016, ADR-0018 (the speech part), ADR-0020, ADR-0022, ADR-0023 and ADR-0024: those records are kept as history and each carries a note. **The `speech_evaluation` gate is ON** since the final acceptance (reports/ai/speech-evaluation-acceptance.json); the evidence is synthetic, AI-written and AI-reviewed, scored once per set against criteria fixed beforehand (`docs/research/gemma_evaluator_results.md`), and the real Quest and ASR validation is DC-10 (Sannan).

## Context
* Between ADR-0012 and ADR-0024 the speech path grew into a stack of corrections around a sentence-embedding and TF-IDF classifier: two heads that had to agree, a critical-mass cap, critical-content cues, an assertion (negation) layer, a validation layer, a clarification policy, and then an optional language-model reader whose answer could be vetoed by all of them. Each layer was added to repair a false credit of the layer before. The controlled development set (493 items) and the experiment of 21 Oct 2026 showed the pattern: a model that reads the sentence with its context (who spoke, whether it happened now, whether it was said as a fact, a plan, a hypothesis or a report) made far fewer semantic errors than the stack, and the stack was the main source of lost legitimate credit.
* The things that must stay deterministic are not semantic. Whether a critical physical action happened, whether the right object, patient or equipment was involved, which scenario step is running, whether a patient question is waiting, and the points themselves are facts and arithmetic the engine owns (ADR-0005).

## Decisions
1. **Pipeline.** ASR transcript → `POST /evaluate/organisations/{id}` (Gemma `gemma4:31b` on Ollama Cloud, free plan, ADR-0024's platform decisions stay: direct API or signed-in local relay, no structured-output support so the reply is validated strictly) → the **judgement** → Unity applies one `speech_evidence` action per judged objective → the engine checks the judgement against its own state → deterministic score → VR feedback. The route returns a judgement and never a score, credit or confidence field.
2. **Gemma judges meaning only.** The prompt (`server/evaluate/prompt.py`, version `gemma-eval-4`, hash pinned in `server/tests/test_evaluate.py`) forces the order: (1) who performed or spoke, (2) whether it happened now, (3) assertion and modality (performed, negated, intended later, hypothetical, reported, another actor, an earlier claim, uncertain, incomplete, none) and form (statement, question, request), (4) an objective only when the trainee actually performed it, (5) every justified objective of a multi-intent utterance (at most three), (6) relevance, correctness and completeness per performed objective on a 0 to 3 ordinal scale with written anchors, (7) any claim about a physical or simulator-verifiable action, recorded as a claim and never assumed true. The approved annotation rubric rules are in the prompt verbatim; the worked examples are original contrast pairs, not rows of any dataset (a test enforces this). There are no lexical negation vetoes, no classifier fallbacks, no semantic caps and no agreement requirement: when the model is wrong the remedy is the prompt or the definitions, not a downstream correction.
3. **Strict contract** (`server/evaluate/contract.py`). Closed vocabularies, grades 0 to 3, evidence quoted from the utterance, `performed_now` equal to (assertion is performed and actor is the trainee); anything else is rejected, never repaired. One bounded retry for a format problem or transport error (`server/evaluate/reader.py`, two attempts, 25 s total, no retry on 401, 402, 403 or 429). When no valid judgement results, the answer is UNAVAILABLE with the menu of the existing verified actions and **no speech credit**; the trainee is never blocked.
4. **The simulator is authoritative.** The request carries the engine's `InterpretationContext` (running steps, eligible objectives, waiting question or read-back, recent turns, verified actions). Gemma never decides a simulator fact. In the engine (`src/ProcSense.Core/Scoring/Evidence.cs`) a judgement is accepted as communication evidence only if it is well formed, the trainee performed it now, a step is running, the behaviour's window contains that step, ANSWER_QUESTION has a waiting patient question and CLOSED_LOOP a waiting read-back, the grade is above the credit floor, and the utterance and objective were not already judged. One best graded credit counts per behaviour.
5. **Critical actions never get physical completion from speech.** A critical objective (identity, allergy, consent, handover element) is graded for a small communication credit (weight 0.25) and is never accepted as evidence for its behaviour. The physical part comes only from deterministic accepted actions named in `content/assessment/mapping.json` (`critical_act_evidence`: CONFIRM_IDENTITY by the identity check of b13, STATE_ALLERGY by the allergy questions of b02, CONSENT_SIGN by the consent signature of b21, HANDOVER_CRITICAL_ELEMENT and SBAR_ELEMENT by the handover actions of b11). A spoken claim of an action without that evidence earns no physical component.
6. **Graded communication score (EXPERIMENTAL rubric, `content/scoring/rubric.json`, key `communication`, generated by `tools/content/build_scoring.py`, easy to change).** For a performed objective with relevance r, correctness c, completeness k (each 0 to 3): grade = 0 if r = 0 or c = 0, otherwise (r/3) × (c/3) × (0.4 + 0.6 × k/3); a grade under the credit floor 0.1 is not credited. A behaviour's communication credit is its best grade times the weight of the objective (ordinary 1.0, critical 0.25) times the behaviour's existing scoring weight (`behaviour_weights` of the scoring tables); the report adds a Communication list and total to the process total, and the pass/fail verdict does not change. The weights derive from the existing scoring points; no clinical requirement was added. The expected behaviours hold by construction: irrelevant or no act gives about zero, negated, intended later, hypothetical, reported and another actor's acts give zero current performance credit, incomplete gives partial credit, correct but incomplete gives substantial partial credit, complete gives full credit, and a verified action plus the right words combine. The Python mirror in `tools/synth_dev/evaluator_metrics.py` equals the engine on every combination (`tools/ci/test_evaluate_parity.py`).
7. **Retired and removed** (code, tests, configuration and heads): the sentence-embedding and TF-IDF classifier and both heads, the two-head agreement gate, the critical-mass cap and its policies, the critical-content cues, the assertion (negation) layer, the interpretation service and its validation, clarification and fallback logic, the earlier optional semantic reader and its critical verifier, the per-act thresholds, the classifier benchmark, safety-gate, recall, cap, NLI and rubric harnesses, the classifier safety scenarios and their mutation tests, the `/classify` and `/interpret` routes, the `classifier*` and `speech_interpretation` gate features and their suites. `server/retrieval/embedder.py` keeps the one piece still used elsewhere (the Ollama embedder of the fact-retrieval evaluation).
8. **Retained.** Everything that is authoritative or not about classification: the scenario and clinical content, the taxonomy and mapping, the engine's state and evidence checks, physical-action verification, the process scoring, the frozen unsafe-speech sets and their hash protection (the sets and the recorded final results; the retired candidate's code hashes remain in the record as history), every result file and research page as history, the ASR infrastructure and speech-order handling, the development set and its tooling, the experiment folder.
9. **Gate.** `speech_evaluation` in `content/ai/thresholds.json` reads `reports/ai/speech-evaluation-acceptance.json` (written by `tools/eval/speech_acceptance.py` from the two stored acceptance reports): 0 critical physical completions, 0 window/state bypass, recall at least 0.90, precision at least 0.95, performed versus not performed at least 0.95, valid replies at least 0.995, non-performed credit at most 1 percent, unjustified ordinary credit on critical content at most 3 percent, mixed items credited, multi-intent recall at least 0.90, contrast sets at least 0.85, partial-credit bands at least 0.80, no reply rejected for long evidence, median at most 4 s. The thresholds were proposed in DT-245 and fixed in the acceptance criteria written before the runs; the owner accepted the criteria and the result (22 Oct 2026). With no model host configured the route answers the menu.

## Consequences
* Fewer layers, one place where meaning is decided (the prompt and the written definitions) and one where credit is decided (the engine). A semantic failure is now visible as a model judgement that can be read, tested and improved, not hidden by a later veto.
* Safety no longer rests on keyword guards. It rests on the engine: the evaluator cannot grant physical credit, a critical objective cannot be accepted from speech, and an unavailable or invalid judgement credits nothing. The residual risk is a model that names an ordinary objective for critical content: it earns at most the ordinary objective's credit in its own window, never the critical behaviour. 4 such cases occurred in 91 critical items in development.
* Cost and speed: about 5,000 tokens and 2.2 s median (5.8 s p95) per utterance on the free plan; the live path is no longer under 300 ms, so Unity must keep the existing menu and not block on the answer (hook 13).
* The decision to depend on a cloud model for the credit path (privacy of the transcript, DT-248; availability; quota) stays with the owner.

## Alternatives considered
* Keep the stack and add the model as another voter: it keeps every layer that lost legitimate credit and makes failures harder to attribute. Rejected.
* Let Gemma return points: arbitrary, unstable and impossible to audit. Rejected; it returns ordinal grades only.
* Let Gemma decide physical completion: contradicts ADR-0005. Rejected.

## Final acceptance on a fresh held-out set (21 October 2026)

The owner asked for final acceptance on a fresh frozen held-out set and, if it met the acceptance criteria, for the feature to be approved and closed. The set (`tests/synthetic_heldout_v1`, 259 items and a 32-item partial-credit addendum; reserved personas P5 and P7, four reserved topic groups; specifications, data, prompt `gemma-eval-4`, code and criteria hashed before the single scored run) is AI-written and AI-reviewed, so the result is acceptance evidence for the software and not independent validation. Result: critical physical completions from speech 0; legitimate recall 98/102 (96.1%); performed versus not performed 97.3%; valid replies 99.6%; partial-credit behaviour 27/32 in the expected band; median response 2.7 s. **Credited precision was 92.5% against the proposed 0.95, 2 non-performed items earned credit against the proposed 0, and 4 critical-content items earned an ordinary credit against the proposed 0.** By the pre-registered rule (blocking criteria pass, others missed) the verdict is **NOT APPROVED, owner decision (DT-246)**: this ADR is not marked complete, no plan or feature was ticked, and the evaluator, prompt and criteria were not changed. The held-out set must not be scored again. The real Meta Quest and ASR end-to-end validation was added to Sannan's checklist as DC-10 and does not block the software decision. Full table and the reasons: `docs/research/gemma_evaluator_results.md`.

## Criteria for held-out v2 (written 21 October 2026, before v2 existed)

Owner decision after held-out v1: option 2, ONE final prompt-level refinement (prompt `gemma-eval-5`, general categories and the approved rubric only, no wording taken from any failed v1 sentence, no lexical veto and no change to the engine or the scoring) and ONE brand-new frozen held-out v2, scored once. **Review of the v1 critical-content category against the approved semantics.** The annotation rubric (rule 1) says identity, allergy and consent questions to the patient win over everything else, and rule 3 makes a handover line one structured act; so a question about reactions to a substance is STATE_ALLERGY only, a request for name and birth date is CONFIRM_IDENTITY only, and the instruction inside a handover line is not also a voice order. Measured against that, the four v1 cases (two allergy questions credited as ASK_TOPIC, an identity request with an EXPLAIN_ACTION added for a timing phrase, a handover line with a VOICE_ORDER added) were unjustified ordinary credits, so the intent of v1's criterion A5 was right. But mentioning a critical topic does not forbid a legitimate ordinary objective: a separate clause that really explains an action, asks about another history topic or gives an order is independent communication and is credited like any other. A flat zero-tolerance count of 'ordinary credit on critical-content items' cannot tell the two apart and, applied to a probabilistic reader, is not a statement about the semantics. **The v2 rules (`tests/synthetic_heldout_v2/acceptance.json`).** D2: an ordinary objective credited on a critical-content item whose specification lists no independent ordinary objective is UNJUSTIFIED; at most 3 percent of the single-critical items (about one in forty). D3: v2 contains mixed items whose specification lists a critical act AND an independent ordinary act performed in separate clauses; the ordinary act must be credited (recall at least 0.80) and the critical behaviour never is. D1: non-performed items (ordinary and critical) that earn any credit at most 1 percent, instead of v1's zero; a negated, planned, hypothetical, reported or other-actor sentence still earns nothing, but one error in a hundred is not read as a failure of the architecture. B1 (critical physical completions = 0) and B2 (the objective a specification places out of its window or without its needed waiting state never earns credit) stay absolute and blocking because the engine, not the model, enforces them; a different objective that really is in window for the same words is judged by precision, not by B2. The core targets are unchanged: recall at least 0.90, credited precision at least 0.95, performed versus not performed at least 0.95, valid replies at least 0.995, partial-credit band accuracy at least 0.80, median response at most 4 s, multi-intent per-act recall at least 0.90 with at most one item with an extra credit, contrast sets fully correct at least 0.85. If a blocking criterion fails the result is BLOCKED; if only others miss it is NOT APPROVED and the owner decides; v2 is not scored a second time and no v3 is created automatically.

## Final acceptance on held-out v2 (21 October 2026)

After the prompt refinement (`gemma-eval-5`: second-objective test, boundary decisions from the approved rubric, critical-objective precedence, 13 original examples; no lexical veto, no engine or scoring change) the architecture was scored once on a brand-new frozen held-out set (`tests/synthetic_heldout_v2`, 244 items and a 32-item partial-credit addendum, prompt, code, data and criteria hashed before the run, no copy of any committed test sentence). Result against the criteria written before v2 existed: critical physical completions 0 and window/state bypass 0 (blocking, passed); recall 99.0%, precision 100%, performed versus not performed 99.2%, valid replies 100%, median 2.3 s, non-performed credits 0 of 95, unjustified ordinary credit on critical content 0 of 40, mixed critical plus ordinary items 8/8, multi-intent 40/40, contrast sets 19/19 (all passed); **controlled partial-credit items in their expected band 25/32 = 78.1% against 0.80 (missed by one item)**. By the pre-registered rule the verdict is **NOT APPROVED, owner decision (DT-246, DT-247)**: this ADR is not marked complete and nothing was ticked. Three of the seven misses were long full-credit sentences whose replies the strict contract rejected because the quoted evidence exceeded 160 characters; two were wrong-content sentences graded correct; two were vague sentences graded complete. v2 is not scored again, no v3 was created and the evaluator, prompt, criteria and scoring were not changed. Table and reasons: `docs/research/gemma_evaluator_results.md`. The real Meta Quest and ASR validation stays DC-10 in Sannan's checklist.

## Final approval (22 October 2026)

Owner decision after held-out v2: the semantic architecture, the `gemma-eval-5` prompt, the scoring semantics and the thresholds are frozen, and the v2 semantic results (recall 99.0%, precision 100%, no non-performed or unjustified critical-content credit, 0 critical physical completions, multi-intent 40/40, contrast sets 19/19) are the accepted final held-out evidence. The one criterion v2 missed (partial-credit bands 25/32 against 0.80) had a genuine robustness cause for three of its seven misses: the strict reply contract capped the quoted `evidence` at 160 characters, so a valid evaluation of a longer natural utterance was rejected twice (DT-247). **The fix** is a contract-level change only: `evidence` may be as long as the longest utterance the route accepts (`MAX_EVIDENCE` = 400 characters in `server/evaluate/contract.py`, the same figure as the route's utterance cap and the engine's `MaxSpan` in `SpeechEvidenceSubsystem.cs`), and it must still be words copied from the utterance; the rationale, the grades, the closed vocabularies and every other rule are unchanged, output stays bounded, and short replies are judged exactly as before (tests in `server/tests/test_evaluate.py` and `InterpretationContextTests.cs`, a mutation test of the cap). The prompt text and hash (`5cf37bc78515`) did not change. **The check** is a fresh frozen 32-item partial-credit set (`tests/synthetic_partial_v3`: full, partial, wrong and irrelevant communication, 10 long or rambling items, no reuse of any earlier sentence), its criteria (at least 80 percent in band; no reply rejected for long evidence), the prompt, the contract and the data hashed before the run, scored once: **27/32 = 84.4% in band, 32/32 valid replies (all 10 long items valid), 0 length rejections**. The 5 misses are the known weaknesses: two wrong-content sentences graded as correct and three vague sentences graded complete. **Decision: the feature is APPROVED.** s22 and the relevant plans, tasks and feature statuses are closed, C37 is set to complete, the `speech_evaluation` gate is ON from the acceptance record, and the final architecture is: **ASR -> Gemma semantic evaluation -> authoritative simulator state/evidence -> deterministic graded scoring -> VR feedback.** What the approval is not: independent human validation, real audio or a headset result. The real Meta Quest and ASR end-to-end validation stays DC-10 in Sannan's checklist; sending real trainee speech to Ollama Cloud still waits for the privacy decision (DT-248); the taxonomy boundaries remain the owner's (DT-241 to DT-244). This feature is not optimised or extended further without a new decision.
