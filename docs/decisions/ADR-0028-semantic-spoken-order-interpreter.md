# ADR-0028 — Spoken orders are read by one semantic call of Gemma in the scenario context; the engine, the read-back and the trainee's yes decide what happens

**Date:** 26 October 2026 · **Status:** ACCEPTED for engineering (owner request of 25 Oct 2026: "follow our existing voice-AI architecture decision … remove unnecessary semantic restrictions; for example a sentence beginning 'Can I…' should not automatically be rejected as an order"). **Activation, 26 Oct 2026:** the project owner reviewed the evaluation and its limits and accepted the synthetic evaluation as sufficient for the controlled demonstration of the final-year project; `order_fallback` is ON for that demonstration only (see Activation below).

## Context
* The spoken-order path (features B23, C32) was a stack of corrections around a small local model, the same shape ADR-0025 removed from speech evaluation: a word-list guard that refused any sentence containing "can i", "could", "should" and similar (DT-208), trigger words and a fuzzy match that had to agree, a model that first chose a *kind* and then an *item* from a short list, a second reading, and a re-ranker. Polite requests ("can I get oxygen on her") were lost, and the guard still could not tell "should I hold the metformin" (a question) from "hold the metformin" (an order) by meaning.
* Measured on sets written by other models the stack reached 90.2 and 72.6 percent (a580f109); the misses were the guard, sentences naming no item and arguably mislabelled non-orders.

## Decision
1. **One semantic call.** `server/orders/interpreter.py` sends the whole sentence, the scenario context (the patient, what is ordered or given, the previous turns, a read-back waiting) and the **approved action catalogue** (every test, medicine, place and staff role with a plain-words gloss, plus the home medicines the patient may take) to Gemma 4 31B Cloud (`prompts/orders/interpret_v1.txt`, route `orders.interpret`, local Qwen as fallback). The answer is structured: `kind` (order, question, hypothetical, negated, ambiguous, incomplete, unsupported, other), `intent`, `target`, `alternatives`, `confidence`, `clarification`.
2. **Meaning, not first words.** The prompt states what each kind of sentence is, with examples of the hard ones: permission-style questions that are requests, questions put to a team member, corrections and short follow-ups, first-person actions, negated and hypothetical sentences, and "a general word for a group of items is ambiguous: ask, do not choose".
3. **Removed from the model path:** the question/condition word list, the trigger-word requirement, the fuzzy agreement rule, the kind stage, the second reading, the re-ranker and the confidence-free single pick. They remain only in the **offline** path: when the model is unavailable (outage, no consent, gate OFF) `ReadBack.hear` falls back to the grammar *with* its question guard, because without a reader of meaning a refusal is the safe answer.
4. **Execution safety is not semantic and is kept.** The model never executes anything. A proposal must name an id of the catalogue and an intent that fits the item's kind (else "unsupported"); a low confidence or model-named alternatives become a question; ambiguous and incomplete sentences ask; a dose is stripped before read-back; the read-back is released only by the trainee's own yes through a signed, single-use, per-user ticket; the engine still validates the verb against the scenario state, required parameters and the scoring rules (a wrong or unsafe request is refused or penalised there, not by the interpreter); consent and approval gating, the one cloud slot, the 4 s live budget and the local fallback of ADR-0026/0027 apply unchanged.
5. **Evaluation.** AI-assistant-authored synthetic sets (`tests/orders/ai_authored/`): a development set (153 rows) used to write and adjust the prompt, and a separate held-out set (151 rows) scored once on the frozen prompt and interpreter (hashes in the report). A model that did not answer (limit or outage) aborts the run; it is never counted. Labels were not changed after a held-out prediction was seen.

## Consequences
* Engineering acceptance (at least 95 percent, no wrong item above 5 percent, non-orders offered at most 10 percent, no confirmation bypass, p95 under 3 s) is met on the AI-assistant-authored held-out set (`reports/ai/orders-ai-heldout_v1.json`). The sets are AI-authored synthetic data, so the human-authored requirement of EX-18 is **not** met: it was waived for the demonstration by the owner (see Activation), not completed.
* Residual error is the model choosing a plausible item for a sentence that names a group ("start the drip", "get some sugar into him", "draw up") instead of asking. The read-back and the yes catch it; it is counted as a safety-related incorrect proposal when the item is a rescue drug or a glucose.
* Cost: one cloud call per spoken order (about 0.8 s median on the free plan); the consent and privacy position of ADR-0027 is unchanged.

## Activation (owner decision of 26 Oct 2026, UTC)
* **Decision.** After personally reviewing the held-out result (151 sentences, 97.4 percent as labelled, four incorrect interpretations, two of them safety-related over-proposals, no confirmation bypass) and the limits of AI-authored synthetic data, the project owner accepted the synthetic evaluation as sufficient for the controlled FYP demonstration and authorised switching `order_fallback` ON for it. The decision is recorded in `reports/ai/ex18-owner-waiver.json`; the gate suite `order_fallback.owner_acceptance_for_demonstration` reads it in place of the earlier suite that demanded a human-authored set. Independent human-authored testing was **waived for the demonstration; it was not done**. No clinician validated anything, and Sannan's physical headset checks (DC-08, DC-11) have not been run.
* **Scope.** The demonstration only. Real trainee use and any clinical deployment stay subject to the institution's approval (`CLOUD_TRAINEE_DATA_APPROVED`), each trainee's own cloud consent (ADR-0027), the privacy review and a human-authored evaluation set (EX-18) or a new explicit decision by the institution. The waiver file records that none of these has been given.
* **Safeguards, all unchanged.** The trainee's own yes before anything is executed (signed, single-use, per-user ticket); the approved action catalogue and intent-kind validation; ambiguous and incomplete sentences become a question; authoritative engine validation and scoring; consent and privacy routing. The implementation and the prompt were not changed for the activation: it is the frozen artefact that was evaluated. Nothing was loosened to raise coverage.
* **One fix made while verifying the consent boundary.** The `/hear` route did not read the person's stored cloud consent (the dialogue, speech-action and evaluation routes do), so a consenting trainee would always have been served by the local model. It now sets the consent for the call like the other routes; with no consent, or none recorded, nothing changes: the words stay local.
* **Only the evaluated model counts.** What the owner accepted is Gemma reading the sentence. The same interpreter with the local model scored 69.5 percent with 13 percent wrong items on the same sentences, so the route (`CloudOnlyInterpreter`, `server/routers/orders.py`) uses the interpreter's answer only when the cloud model gave it; with no approval, no consent, an outage or a slow answer the grammar with its question guard answers, as decision 3 says. In the shipped configuration cloud routing and trainee-data approval are OFF (`CLOUD_ROUTING_ENABLED`, `CLOUD_TRAINEE_DATA_APPROVED`), so spoken orders use the grammar until the operator sets them. **For the demonstration with synthetic data only** set `CLOUD_ROUTING_ENABLED=1` and `CLOUD_SYNTHETIC_DEPLOYMENT=1` (a synthetic deployment; never on a deployment real trainees use). For any real trainee set `CLOUD_TRAINEE_DATA_APPROVED=1` after the institution's approval; each person's own consent is then also required.
* **Known limitation, not hidden.** The four held-out misses are vague sentences ("start the drip", "get some sugar into him", "draw up", "same for the bisoprolol") for which the model chose the most plausible item instead of asking. The read-back and the yes catch them, and they are the starting material for the next development round. Changing the prompt to remove them would produce a different artefact that has not been evaluated and would need a new reserved held-out set; it was deliberately not done as part of the activation.

## Amendment of 27 Oct 2026: Gemma Cloud first in the demonstration configuration, the grammar only as the fallback
* **Owner request.** Make Gemma 4 31B Cloud the default and preferred reader of spoken orders in the dedicated synthetic FYP demonstration configuration, with the offline grammar used only as a fallback, and show which system answered.
* **The profile.** `server/demo/synthetic-fyp-demo.env` (non-secret; `task server-demo`, or `docker run --env-file`) sets `CLOUD_ROUTING_ENABLED=1` and `CLOUD_SYNTHETIC_DEPLOYMENT=1`, `CLOUD_TRAINEE_DATA_APPROVED=0`, `APP_ENV=dev`. It is the only place these are turned on: the defaults in `server/config.py` and `server/.env.example` stay off, and the Dockerfile and the workflows do not set them. A service that starts with the synthetic declaration beside `APP_ENV=prod` or `CLOUD_TRAINEE_DATA_APPROVED=1` **refuses to start** (`server/config.py`). The institutional approval and each person's own consent are untouched: in a deployment that is not declared synthetic, the cloud is used only with the approval and that person's consent.
* **Routing.** `server/orders/cloud_interpreter.py` (`CloudOnlyInterpreter`) calls the gateway in `cloud_only` mode: a route that is not cloud (switch off, no approval, no consent) makes **no model call at all**, and a failed cloud call has **no local second leg** (the local model's reading was never the accepted one). The cloud call has the live budget of 4 s. Any of these returns the answer to the grammar with its question guard. The frozen prompt and `server/orders/interpreter.py` are unchanged.
* **Which system answered.** Every `/hear` answer carries `interpreter` (`gemma_cloud` | `offline_grammar`), `interpreter_model` (the model asked), `interpreter_reported_model` (the model name the **host** put in its own reply: `gemma4:31b` for the relayed Gemma 4 31B Cloud, evidence that the cloud model answered and not only that a flag is on), `fallback_reason` and `interpreter_ms`. The reasons are `CLOUD_ROUTING_OFF`, `PRIVACY_GATE`, `NO_CONSENT`, `CLOUD_BUSY`, `CLOUD_TIMEOUT`, `CLOUD_UNREACHABLE`, `CLOUD_LIMIT`, `CLOUD_INVALID`, `MODEL_BUSY`, `ORDER_INTERPRETER_OFF`, `NO_MODEL_HOST`. The audit row records the reason (never the words) and the service log has one line per order with the same fields. `/ai/health` shows `routing.spoken_orders` (preferred model, active reader, profile) before any call.
* **Unchanged.** The approved catalogue and intent validation, clarification of ambiguous orders (no ticket is made for them), the signed single-use confirmation ticket released only by the trainee's yes, authoritative engine validation and scoring.
* **Checked end to end.** `task demo-orders-check` (`tools/demo/orders_routing_check.py`) sends invented sentences to the real Gemma 4 31B Cloud and records the answering system per sentence in `reports/ai/demo-orders-routing.json`, then proves each fallback (no personal consent, no approval, routing off, unreachable host, timeout) through the same code path. The server tests cover the same paths with a fake host; a live test (`PROCSENSE_LIVE_CLOUD=1`) drives the whole route with the real model.
* **Not done, said plainly.** No Unity script calls the order route yet: the headset's spoken path uses the speech-action route of ADR-0027 for tablet actions, so the Quest UI cannot show the interpreter of spoken orders. The route answer, the log, the audit and `/ai/health` do. The Quest checks (DC-08, DC-11) have not been run; EX-18 is waived for the demonstration, not done.
