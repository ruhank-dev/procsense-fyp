# ADR-0013 — The classifier is evaluated on AI-judged synthetic sets while no people are available; the result changes the guards, not the claim

> **Superseded as architecture by [ADR-0025](ADR-0025-gemma-first-speech-evaluation.md) (21 Oct 2026).** The classifier, gate, cap, cue, assertion and interpretation machinery this record describes was retired and removed; the record and its measurements are kept as history.

**Date:** 18 October 2026 · **Status:** Accepted. Supplements ADR-0012; it does not replace the independent-set requirement.

## Context
The owner could not arrange human authors, labellers or reviewers, and asked for the classifier work to go on without blocking on them: a much more varied synthetic set, an AI judge with a written rubric, agreement between judge passes, thresholds tuned on development data only, the test split scored once, TF-IDF and the Qwen classifier compared with the embedding head, adversarial and regression tests for critical speech, and the result recorded as a synthetic, AI-judged evaluation, never as validation.

## Decision
1. **Sets.** `tests/classifier_synthetic_v2` (840 + 420 + 600 sentences from 12 simulated author profiles, train, dev and test sharing no profile and no scenario group; negation, correction, incomplete speech, ambiguity, recogniser-style noise) and `tests/classifier_synthetic_v3` (300 fresh sentences, 3 new profiles, 2 new scenario groups, written and judged after the v2 test had been scored once, to measure changes made after that look). Sentences were committed before any model scored dev or test; labels change only by a recorded adjudication.
2. **Judging.** Two configurations of one local model (qwen3.5:4b; J1 zero-shot, J2 reworded rubric with examples, shuffled label order and sampling) label every dev and test sentence blind from a written rubric (`rubric.md`, the tie-break order of the annotation guide). A row is certain when both agree with the author's intent, uncertain when the judges split, contested when both agree against it; contested rows are settled in `adjudication.txt`. Kappa between the two configurations: 0.84 (dev), 0.85 (test), 0.81 (v3). **The adjudicator is the same builder that wrote the sentences and sided with the intent in 137 of 139 (v2) and 45 of 46 (v3) cases; this is not an independent check, and agreement between two configurations of one model is not proof that a label is right.** Training sentences were not judged (their label is the author's intent; the manifest says `intent_only`).
3. **Protocol.** Hyperparameter C and the per-act credit thresholds are chosen on v2 dev only; the v2 test is scored once (a lock file refuses a second run); post-hoc figures are labelled as such; v3 is scored once per head.
4. **Result.** v2 test (scored once): TF-IDF macro-F1 0.816 (0.781 to 0.845), embeddings 0.792 (0.757 to 0.822), Qwen 0.654 (0.618 to 0.684); TF-IDF minus embeddings 0.024 (-0.014 to 0.061), not separated. v3: shipped embedding head 0.842, TF-IDF 0.892, the head shipped on 17 October 0.742. Critical acts credited from speech: 0 for every candidate everywhere. The critical sentences credited under an ordinary act: v2 test as scored TF-IDF 2, embeddings 5, Qwen 10.
5. **Changes made because of it.** The head is retrained on 1,940 rows; a critical-mass cap refuses ordinary credit when the head gives a critical act 2 percent or more (chosen on v2 dev only; `tools/bench/tune_mass.py`); the regular-expression guard grew to version 3 from evasive critical sentences (`adversarial_critical.json`, 71 sentences, written before any v2 benchmark). Effect, post hoc on the v2 test: credited 346 to 266, precision 0.858 to 0.902, critical sentences under an ordinary act 5 to 0. On the fresh v3: credited 137 of 300, precision 0.942, 0 critical sentences under an ordinary act (two late allergy cues were added after the one remaining case was seen, so that figure is post hoc). Evasive sentences credited under an ordinary act: 8 of 71 and 7 of 48 before, 0 and 0 after (the 48 were written by the same author before the change, so they show generalisation only to that author's other wordings).
6. **The default stays embeddings; the feature stays off.** The owner chose the embedding head as the provisional classifier and the gate's `independent_evidence` suite (and the independent set it needs) cannot pass on synthetic data, by design. `classifier_synthetic2` records the AI-judged results for information.

## Update, 18 October 2026 (second session): the guard measured on its own, and fresh evasive sentences

The "0 and 0" figures above are on sentences the cues were chosen from (the tuning set) or that were looked at afterwards, so they say little about new wording. Two further sets were written and kept frozen (`tests/classifier_synthetic_v2/adversarial_manifest.json`, checked in CI): hold-out 2 (60 sentences, written before cues version 4) and hold-out 3 (48, written after it; no cue was chosen from it). Findings, all synthetic, same author, not independent:

* **The cue list alone does not generalise.** `tools/bench/guard_eval.py` runs the guard with no classifier: on hold-out 2 it caught 23 of 60 before version 4 and 59 of 60 after (tuning evidence by then); on the fresh hold-out 3 it catches 6 of 48 (13 percent). A list of phrases cannot cover new wording; it is a floor, not the protection.
* **The full path (head, critical-mass cap, cues) is not perfect on fresh wording.** Hold-out 2 before the cue change: 2 of 60 critical sentences credited under an ordinary act (a consent question and a request in SBAR form); after, 0. Hold-out 3, fresh: 3 of 48 (an identity request, a fasting sentence, an SBAR request), shipped head. That is 6 percent, not 0.
* **What stays true by construction.** No critical act is credited from speech (`ActClassifier.credit_allowed`, replayed in `tools/ci/test_guard_eval.py` with a classifier that always answers an ordinary act at confidence 1.0): the failure is credit for an ordinary communication behaviour on a critical sentence, never credit for a critical action; the engine's action path decides critical items (ADR-0005).
* **Not done:** retraining the head on the adversarial sentences (it would consume the sets as evidence; it needs a new fresh set to judge it) and a semantic second guard. Both are listed as the next step if the classifier is ever to be enabled; the gate stays off.

## Alternatives
Switch the default to TF-IDF now (two synthetic sets say it is equal or better, 0.8 ms, no service call, and it reads recogniser noise better: 83.8 against 76.7 percent): not done because the synthetic sets are written by one author, lexical overlap with the training styles favours TF-IDF, and the question is open until real speech exists; a pure-Python TF-IDF head is therefore available as the backend `tfidf` (`CLASSIFIER_BACKEND=tfidf`, `server/classify/tfidf.py`, `content/assessment/act_head_tfidf.json`, 2.1 MB, same predictions as scikit-learn to 2e-06): with the same critical-mass rule (zero critical sentences credited under an ordinary act on v2 dev) its cap is 0.01, which leaves it crediting 53 of 300 on v3 at precision 0.981 and 103 of 600 on the v2 test (post hoc) against 137 and 266 for the embedding head, so it is the safer but less generous option; it stays an alternative, not the default. Hand-label by the builder alone: rejected, it would be the same model grading its own work with no second reading. Treat judge agreement as validation: rejected.

## Consequences
The classifier work is no longer blocked, and the guards against crediting critical speech are stronger and measured. The claim does not change: nothing here is evidence about real trainee speech. The adjudication being non-independent is stated in the manifest, the README, this ADR and the evaluation report.

## Revisit if
The independent set exists (re-run all three candidates on it; switch the default if TF-IDF is still equal or better); a person reviews the adjudications; the guard's cost (credits removed from harmless sentences, 21 to 23 percent of credits on dev and v3) proves too high in real use.
