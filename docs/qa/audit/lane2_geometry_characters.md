## 2. Why the optimised city is slower on this desktop (what is known, ruled out, open)

Measured per view (`v320.visible.csv`, renderers and triangles of the city materials, B20 against B28 at 320 m): V2 30 to 54 renderers, 321k to 175k triangles; V10 26 to 34, 287k to 173k; V3 32 to 37, 321k to 138k; V7 28 to 23, 321k to 112k. At 80 m: V2 30 to 76, V10 26 to 49 (more renderers, same triangles).

* **Ruled out: triangles** (fewer everywhere, except the theatre cell, below).
* **Ruled out: chunk count.** 2000 m cells (20 renderers) are not better than 320 m (44) and the loss at 80 m (401) is only about 2% larger.
* **Ruled out: SetPass.** The 201 against 214 difference is baked occlusion (B30, occlusion on, has 204), not the city.
* **Ruled out: culling script cost as the main driver on its own:** the fast path made no measurable difference (15.41 against 15.55 ms). Probable reason, found while preparing this audit: the pose cache holds one pose, and the benchmark alternates two cameras (the eyes) every frame, so the cache never hits and the culling runs twice per frame. The headset renders one multiview camera and culls once. **`ExteriorVisibility.OnlyCamera` (new, null by default) lets the benchmark cull once per frame** like the headset; re-running B20/B28/B29 with it is requested (`benchmark_requests.md`, lane2). Until that run, the CPU part of the B28/B29 loss is an upper bound that does not apply to the headset.
* **Theatre turn cell: found a cause, in the cull cache (fixed in code, not yet re-measured).**
  * What the dump measures: the visible-renderer dump is taken at the **initial pose** of the cell (right after the warm-up loop at `Rot(0)`), after `PerfToggles.Apply` for that configuration; at that pose the camera in the theatre looks at +x, no aperture is in view, so `ExteriorVisibility` must hide the whole city for **both** variants (`hide` is variant-independent: `ExteriorVisibility.cs` lines 136 and 137).
  * What went wrong: `PerfToggles.Apply` rewrites `forceRenderingOff` of **every** renderer (including the city) on each configuration and cell change, while `ExteriorVisibility` (fast path, round 3) trusts a cached state: `MarkDirty` only re-read the flags (`Resync`), and `Apply(hide, portal)` then returned early because the hide code equalled the cached `_applied` code. The city stayed visible although `hide` was true until the code changed (a head turn that brings an aperture into view and back). Whether a configuration drew the stale city depended on what the previous configuration and cell had left in `_applied`, so the benchmark's interleaved order made B28/B29 (and B20) differ by accident. B28's 43 visible chunks (184k triangles) at that pose are the stale chunks; B20 happened to be applied from a different `_applied` state.
  * Fix: `Resync()` now also sets `_applied = -1`, so every re-read of the flags is followed by a re-apply (the write is guarded by the cached `_off` comparison, so the cost is a loop of comparisons every 30 frames). Production is not affected by the original problem (nothing else writes the city's `forceRenderingOff` on the Quest), so this is a benchmark and diagnostic correctness bug, but it **invalidates the per-cell comparison of B20 with B28/B29 wherever the hide state mattered** (theatre, glass-free rooms): the B28/B29 stereo numbers in `quest2_benchmark_method.md` (+3.4% to +5%) were taken with the stale city drawn in some cells, so they are an upper bound until re-run. The averaged theatre turn cell triangles (profiler counter, whole 60-frame turn) still show B28 at 1,358k against B20 at 1,573k (-14%), i.e. the stale state was not dominant there, but its effect on the per-config totals is unknown.
  * Guard and probe: `TheatreCellProbe` (new, `Assets/Tests/PlayMode/TheatreCellProbe.cs`) has `Stale_flags_are_reapplied` (regression test: rewrite the city flags from outside, mark dirty, render once, the city must be hidden again) and `Probe_theatre` (csv of decision, room index, chunks drawn, city renderers not forced off and visible, right after the configuration change, after warm-up and through the turn, for B20, B28, B15, B27). Registered under `extra_tests` in `benchmark_requests.md` for lane 4's next launch. **Neither is compiled or run yet** (no Unity launch from this lane).
* **Open (needs lane 4's batch):** the clean pair comparison B8 (city hidden) against B20 and B17 (occlusion on) and B55 against B15 and B27 (occlusion off): that yields the cost of each city variant as a difference from no city, with intervals.

## 3. Geometry composition (authoritative scene data, `lane2_geometry_data.md`, generated by `GeometryAudit` on 10 Nov 2026)

| Scene | Interior mesh renderers | Interior triangles (all drawn) | Skinned (triangles) | Readable interior meshes | BatchingStatic | Materials with instancing on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PatientBay | 273 | 263,628 | 14 (130,714) | 16 of 170 | 127 | 77 of 102 |
| Corridor | 636 | 195,866 | 17 (92,092) | 35 of 134 | 189 | 73 of 95 |
| OT | 208 | 72,592 | 2 (17,390) | 7 of 63 | 188 | 35 of 49 |

* **The city is the single largest triangle block in the corridor scene:** original city 180,654 triangles in **5 renderers** (4 materials); optimised city (320 m cells) 131,938 triangles in **44 renderers** (-27% triangles, +39 renderers). Neither variant casts shadows, has colliders or readable meshes; all renderers are BatchingStatic and none is an occludee (so the baked occlusion data never culls either city; the earlier idea that B20's occlusion hides the city in the theatre is wrong).
* **This explains the desktop result better than anything else:** the original city is only five draws; the optimised city saves at most 48k triangles per eye and adds 39 renderers, each with its own culling decision and submit cost. At this resolution the desktop card is limited by batches and CPU work, not by 48k triangles. A variant with the same five renderers and fewer triangles (no chunking, so no cone culling) would remove the extra draws; the 2000 m variant (20 renderers) was still +2.9%, so something other than the renderer count also differs (open: mesh vertex layout and vertex-cache order of the simplified meshes; needs a per-variant mesh dump).
* Skinned characters are about 92k to 131k triangles per scene (patient 26.8k triangles, 53 bones, 27 blendshapes; hipoly staff 8k to 9k, 80 bones, 175 blendshapes). In the bay every skinned renderer has `updateWhenOffscreen = true`; the character culler sets it false with locked bounds (the corridor and OT already have false).
* Interior props are modest: the heaviest meshes are 4.5k to 17k triangles each (wheelchair 14,000; bench 5 x 4,488; wall clocks 3,380 x 7), 13 repeated meshes in the corridor (sconces 9, frames 7, door frames 7, 360 "Cube" 12-triangle blocks, 101 of them BatchingStatic). Most repeated meshes already sit on instancing-enabled materials (408 of 451 instances), and the SRP Batcher handles compatible shaders, so GPU instancing adds nothing there (B51 will confirm).
* **Static batching limits:** only 16 of 170 (bay), 35 of 134 (corridor) and 7 of 63 (OT) interior meshes are readable, so runtime static batching (B50) would combine only about 10% to 25% of them; making the rest readable doubles their CPU-side memory. LOD candidates are few (no mesh above 17k triangles); LODs are not worth building without headset evidence.
* The visible-renderer csv files (`v*.visible.csv`) count renderer-material pairs seen by the camera, including shadow-caster and both-eye passes, so their absolute triangle numbers must not be compared with the scene totals above; only same-file B20-against-B28 differences are meaningful.
